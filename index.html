Case 02 · Risk & compliance

Challenging a costly false-positive fraud signal

Estimated impact $200K–$350K potential wrongful losses prevented
01 · Risk

The policy documents resembled a known fraud pattern. Accepting the flag at face value would have produced wrongful account actions.

02 · Judgement

03 · Outcome

The decision was corrected

Confirmed the policies were legitimate, prevented incorrect driver actions and surfaced a review gap worth correcting.

Case 03 · Product initiative · Ongoing

Reframing lower senior usage as an access problem

Lower direct app usage among riders aged 60+ could have been read as lower demand. The booking behaviour pointed instead to friction in how older riders accessed the experience.

Current status Ongoing no launch or outcome metric claimed yet
01 · Signal

Lower direct booking

Usage analysis showed riders aged 60+ were less likely to book through the app directly than younger groups.

02 · Reframe

Need was not the same as usage

Booking behaviour suggested rides were often being arranged on the rider's behalf, making usability and account access the more useful product question.

03 · Response

Design around the friction

Partnered with Engineering around a simplified booking path and dedicated support for login and account setup.

Observe

Age-group usage gap

Reframe

Scope

Simpler flow and support path

Build

Cross-functional work in progress

My ownership: Analysed the usage pattern, brought the finding into a product conversation and helped shape the proposed response. Because the initiative is ongoing, this case demonstrates product judgement and cross-functional execution—not a completed business result.
